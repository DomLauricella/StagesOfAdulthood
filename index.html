EARLY ADULTHOOD

Adulthood doesn't
follow one timeline.

Starting college, changing careers, or moving somewhere new can happen at different times. Through those changes, building close relationships is an important part of development.

THE CORE IDEA

Erikson: intimacy versus isolation

In Erikson's theory, early adulthood centers on forming close, committed relationships. Intimacy involves trust, openness, and mutual care while keeping your own sense of self.

Intimacy

Feeling connected through trust, openness, and mutual support. This can include close friendships as well as romantic relationships.

Example: You can talk honestly with a friend and support each other during a difficult semester.

Isolation

Feeling disconnected or having difficulty forming close relationships. Being surrounded by people does not always mean feeling close to them.

Example: You see classmates every week but feel that you have nobody you can turn to.

A stage is not a deadline. Age and milestones alone do not reveal the quality of someone's relationships. Being single or enjoying time alone does not automatically mean isolation.

PUT THE IDEA INTO PRACTICE

Three paths. The same need for connection.

These are fictional examples. Think about each question, then reveal the connection to Erikson's theory.

01 / EDUCATION

Returning to college

Alex returns to college after several years of work. Alex gets good grades but feels out of place and rarely talks to classmates.

How could Alex build connection beyond simply attending class?

Joining a study group could create chances to get to know others. Over time, sharing concerns and offering support could help those connections grow into close friendships.

The connection: Grades show academic progress. Trust and mutual support tell us more about intimacy.

02 / WORK

Changing careers

Jordan begins a new career and spends most evenings working. Jordan has less time for close friends and stops reaching out.

Why doesn't career success automatically mean Jordan feels connected?

A career can provide purpose, but it does not automatically provide close relationships. Making time to talk honestly with friends could help Jordan maintain support during the change.

The connection: Reaching a work milestone and developing intimacy are different things.

03 / INDEPENDENCE

Moving somewhere new

Sam moves to a new city and lives alone. Sam regularly calls a close friend and slowly gets to know people through a local hobby group.

Does living alone mean Sam is experiencing isolation? Why?

No. Sam has a close friendship and is creating opportunities for new connections. Living arrangements alone cannot tell us whether someone feels emotionally supported.

The connection: Intimacy concerns the closeness of relationships, not whether someone lives with another person.

THE TAKEAWAY

Look at connection, not just milestones.

Two people the same age may take different paths through school, work, and relationships. A degree, a job, or a move does not tell the whole story. To apply intimacy versus isolation, ask whether the person has relationships built on trust, openness, and mutual care.

Think about it: What would you need to know about someone's relationships before deciding how this concept applies?
